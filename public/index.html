            Turning AI off
            
    
            
      Plenty of researchers would rather no language model went near their conclusions. That is a
      reasonable position, not a superstition: a model's judgements are not reproducible the way a
      fixed scoring rule is, they can be confidently wrong, and checking them means re-reading every
      abstract yourself. This switch exists so you can keep the parts of Kanshiki that are
      deterministic and auditable, and leave the rest.
    
            

    
            
      Nothing here is hidden from you either way: the evidence grade never used AI, and every
      model-driven figure is labelled as one. But two things Kanshiki does are model-driven and
      will stop, so this is a real change in what you get back, not a cosmetic setting.
    
            

    
            What you lose
            
    
              
      
            • 
        Direction of the evidence. Whether studies support or contradict a claim is
        judged per study by the model. With it off, the direction badge reads
        "Direction not assessed" and the contradiction spotlight never appears.
      
              • 
      
            • 
        Plain-English summaries. A claim gets a sentence assembled from study
        metadata instead of a written verdict. A single study shows its raw abstract rather than an
        explanation of it.
      
              • 
      
            • 
        Search quality. Your claim is turned into a PubMed query by keyword
        stripping rather than by the model, so the search is broader and cruder, and you may be
        graded on a different, less relevant set of studies.
      
              • 
    
            

    
            What still works, unchanged
            
    
              
      
            • The evidence strength grade, its quality tiers, gates, and score. None of it ever used AI.
              • 
      
            • The evidence-over-time timeline.
              • 
      
            • Retraction detection, and the funding breakdown.
              • 
      
            • Study credibility scoring, citation and venue statistics, and all four citation formats.
